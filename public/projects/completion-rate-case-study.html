Workload Completion

TPD (tickets per day) created pressure for agents to hit a daily threshold, but that threshold depended on a steady flow of incoming work — something the operation could not guarantee. I designed and built a completion-rate model that measures performance against assigned workload, giving teams a fairer and more practical view of output.

01 · The problem

TPD answered the wrong question.

Tickets per day measured absolute output. The issue was that agents could only hit the target if enough suitable work entered the queue in the first place.

TPD

  • Fixed ticket target
  • Dependent on incoming volume
  • Performance signal could be distorted by workload supply
→

Completion Rate

  • Assigned workload
  • Actual responsibility
  • Performance measured against work available to the agent
02 · The solution

A KPI with validation logic behind it.

A task only counts as completed when the recorded activity matches the original allocation. That made the KPI useful not only for scoring performance, but also for diagnosing why work was not recognised as complete.

A task is counted as complete when all four conditions are met:

04 · From KPI to operating model

A clearer role for each metric.

TPD was not discarded. Its purpose changed. Completion Rate became the primary performance KPI, while TPD continued to support capacity planning and forecasting.

06 · The outcome

A sustained improvement in performance.

Since rollout in May 2026, completion rate moved from the 70–80% learning curve to a maintained 95–98%. Leads learned to balance prioritisation and allocation more effectively, while management could focus less on forcing volume and more on work quality and capacity.
